# Pedagogical and Editorial Standards

## Lesson structure

A substantial lesson should contain:
1. title, level, prerequisites and estimated workload;
2. measurable learning objectives;
3. motivation and intuitive explanation;
4. precise definitions and notation;
5. properties and proofs where appropriate;
6. worked examples with explicit reasoning;
7. counterexamples and limits of applicability;
8. applications to statistics, data science, economics or actuarial science where meaningful;
9. software laboratory when it adds value;
10. progressive exercises and complete corrections;
11. common misconceptions;
12. mastery criteria and self-check;
13. traceable references and scope notes.

## Mathematical quality

- State assumptions and domains.
- Distinguish theorem, heuristic, convention and numerical approximation.
- Do not omit important steps in proofs intended for self-study.
- Verify calculations independently, preferably with symbolic and numerical checks where suitable.
- Identify when software uses floating-point approximations or tolerance-based algorithms.
- Include counterexamples to prevent overgeneralization.

## Assessment quality

- Assess the stated objectives, not incidental reading details.
- Use a balanced range of difficulties and cognitive demands.
- Avoid trick questions unless the target is explicitly misconception detection.
- Ensure exactly one correct response for single-choice items.
- Include alternative accepted formulations for open responses.
- Make scoring transparent and reproducible.
- Use independent review for summative exams.

## Editorial states

- `planned`: in roadmap only;
- `draft`: content exists but is not reviewed;
- `reviewed`: mathematical and pedagogical review completed;
- `tested`: rendered and exercised in the application;
- `published`: available to learners;
- `retired`: replaced or withdrawn, with historical attempts preserved.

A source file existing in Git is not enough to label an item published.

## Language and accessibility

French is the initial authoring language. Design translation-ready content structures; do not machine-translate mathematical prose without review. Use descriptive link text, semantic headings, keyboard-accessible interactions, adequate contrast and meaningful alternative text.

## References and scope

Prefer primary textbooks, university course notes, standards bodies and official technical documentation. Cite specific chapters, sections or pages. State where the source was used and distinguish source material from original examples. A reference does not automatically validate every interpretation or exercise.
